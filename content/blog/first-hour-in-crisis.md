When someone you love is in acute distress, the first hour is about safety and clear next steps — not perfect words. This guide walks through what families can do immediately, which verified lines to use, and how to avoid scrambling for outdated clinic numbers.

Start with danger. If someone is violently injured, unconscious, or at immediate risk of harming themselves or others, call [9-1-1](tel:911). Do not wait for a chat tool or a web search to catch up.

If the danger is suicidal crisis or severe emotional distress and you are in Canada or the U.S., call or text [9-8-8](tel:988). In Canada, [988.ca](https://988.ca) explains how the line works. In the U.S., [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org) and [help someone else](https://988lifeline.org/help-someone-else/) offer the same pathway. Stay with the person if it is safe to do so. Speak in short sentences. You do not need a diagnosis to ask for help.

While you wait on the line, gather only what you know: their first name, approximate age, where you are, and whether substances or weapons are involved. Skip inventing a treatment plan. Skip Googling random clinic phones that may be closed.

If the person is in Ontario and the crisis is also about finding mental health or addiction services after the immediate danger passes, [ConnexOntario](https://connexontario.ca) at [1-866-531-2600](tel:18665312600) can help with live referrals. Keep that for after 9-8-8 or 9-1-1 has addressed the acute risk.

Liam's Call is not a crisis line. Use it later to sort through next steps privately — never instead of 9-8-8 or 9-1-1 when someone is in danger.

If you want to keep going privately after the hour settles, you can [talk with Liam's Call AI](https://liamscall.com/) — free, no account required.
