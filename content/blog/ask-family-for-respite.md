Asking family for respite often fails because the ask is vague. This guide offers a clearer request: one time window, one task, one follow-up.

Lead with the need, not the scorecard. “Can you take Saturday 2–6 so I can sleep?” works better than “Nobody ever helps.” The [Ontario Caregiver Organization](https://ontariocaregiver.ca) frames caregiving as shared work — their [get support](https://ontariocaregiver.ca/get-support/) page and helpline ([1-833-227-3778](tel:18332273778)) can help you think through what to ask for.

Name what “done” looks like: meals, medication reminders you have written down, or simply being present. Offer a short handoff note. If they cannot do Saturday, ask which day they can own this month.

If the conversation turns cruel, pause. You can revisit later. Respite can also come from community resources — start with [211](https://211.ca) for local caregiver and community supports, and [NAMI](https://www.nami.org/Your-Journey/Family-Members-and-Caregivers) for family education in the U.S.

If safety collapses while you wait for help, call [9-1-1](tel:911) or [9-8-8](tel:988).
