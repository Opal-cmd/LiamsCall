The first 48 hours after discharge can feel more chaotic than the admission. This guide is a family checklist for those two days — not medical advice, and not a substitute for the discharge plan you were given.

Before leaving the hospital, ask for written next appointments, medication instructions if any were provided, and who to call if symptoms return. Keep that paper where everyone can find it. [CAMH health info](https://www.camh.ca/en/health-info) is a calm place for general mental-health education; your discharge team remains the source for this specific stay.

At home, reduce decisions. One quiet room, regular meals, limited visitors, and sleep where possible. Agree who holds the phone overnight. Do not invent new medications or change doses — that is for clinicians.

If danger returns — suicidal crisis, violence, severe withdrawal — call [9-1-1](tel:911) or [9-8-8](tel:988). In Ontario, [ConnexOntario](https://connexontario.ca) can help with follow-up service navigation after the acute risk is addressed.

Write down what you observe in simple notes: sleep, appetite, mood, whether they took prescribed medication as directed by their care team. Bring those notes to the follow-up visit.
