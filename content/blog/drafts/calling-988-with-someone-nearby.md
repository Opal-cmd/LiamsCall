Calling or texting 9-8-8 while someone you love is in the same room can feel awkward. This guide covers a calm script, what the counsellor needs, and when to switch to 9-1-1 instead.

If there is immediate physical danger, call [9-1-1](tel:911) first. 9-8-8 is for suicidal crisis and severe mental-health distress in Canada and the U.S. — see [988.ca](https://988.ca) or [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org). The page [Help someone else](https://988lifeline.org/help-someone-else/) is written for people in your position.

You can call with the person beside you, or step into a hallway if that keeps everyone safer. You can also text 988 if speaking out loud feels impossible. Say who you are: “I’m calling about my partner / child / parent who is with me and is in crisis.” Share location, whether they are safe right now, and whether they have a plan to harm themselves. You do not need their full medical history.

If they do not want you on the call, you can still contact 988 for guidance on how to support them. Do not invent local counsellor phone numbers. Do not promise outcomes the line cannot guarantee.

After the call, write down any next steps you were given. If Ontario treatment navigation is needed later, [ConnexOntario](https://connexontario.ca) can help with live service matching. Our [Resources](/resources) page lists the same verified directories we maintain on the site.
