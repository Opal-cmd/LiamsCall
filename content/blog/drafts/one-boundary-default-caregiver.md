If you are the default caregiver, every request can feel urgent. This guide helps you set one boundary that holds — not a total life redesign in a single night.

Choose a boundary you can keep for two weeks: one evening off phone duty, one chore you will not own alone, or one weekly hour that stays yours. Say it once, calmly, without a courtroom closing argument. The [Ontario Caregiver Organization](https://ontariocaregiver.ca/get-support/) offers support and a helpline at [1-833-227-3778](tel:18332273778) when you need a steadier place to sort this out.

Expect pushback. A boundary is not a rejection of the person you love; it is a limit on an unsustainable pattern. If siblings share care unevenly, ask for one specific shift rather than a vague “help more.”

If the situation tips into crisis — suicidal distress, violence, overdose — use [9-8-8](tel:988) or [9-1-1](tel:911). Boundaries do not replace emergency response.

Write the boundary down where you can see it. Consistency matters more than eloquence.
