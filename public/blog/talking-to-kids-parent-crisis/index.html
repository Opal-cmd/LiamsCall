Talking to Kids When a Parent Is Using or in Crisis

How to talk to children honestly and safely when a parent faces addiction or housing crisis. Practical language tips and resources like Kids Help Phone.

Published

August 6, 2026

← All stories

When a parent is struggling with addiction, severe mental health challenges, or housing instability, the children in the family feel the weight of the crisis even if they do not fully understand it. This post offers practical guidance on how caregivers, relatives, or supportive adults can talk to kids about these difficult situations using age-appropriate, honest language. We discuss how to validate their feelings, establish safety, and connect them with external support systems like Kids Help Phone when they need someone else to talk to.

Be Honest but Age-Appropriate

Children are highly perceptive and often notice changes in a parent's behavior, mood, or living situation. Attempting to hide a crisis completely can sometimes increase their anxiety, as they may fill in the blanks with their own worst fears. It is usually best to offer a simple, honest explanation that matches their developmental level.

For a younger child, you might explain addiction as a sickness that makes a person behave in ways that are confusing or hurtful. For an older child or teenager, you can use more direct terms while avoiding unnecessary details that might overwhelm them. The goal is to explain the situation without placing blame on the parent or the child.

Reassure Them It Is Not Their Fault

Children often internalize family stress and may secretly believe they caused the parent's substance use or the family's housing instability. It is vital to state clearly and repeatedly that the adult's struggles are not the child's fault, nor is it the child's responsibility to fix them.

You can remind them that they are allowed to love their parent while also feeling angry, sad, or scared about their behavior. Validating these conflicting emotions helps children feel seen and reduces the isolation that often accompanies family crises.

Focus on Safety and Routine

During a housing crisis or a period of active substance use, a child's sense of predictability is shattered. Focus conversations on what is being done to keep them safe. Let them know who will be taking them to school, where they will be sleeping, and who they can turn to if they feel unsafe.

If you are navigating housing instability, resources through the SAMHSA homelessness programs can help you find structural support, which in turn helps you provide a more stable environment for the children in your care.

Connect Them to Outside Support

Sometimes, children and youth find it easier to talk to a trained professional who is outside the immediate family circle. Providing them with resources they can access independently empowers them to seek help when they feel overwhelmed.

In Canada, youth can reach out to Kids Help Phone by calling 1-800-668-6868 or texting CONNECT to 686868 for free, confidential, and professional support. For families navigating these challenges in Ontario, consulting the Centre for Addiction and Mental Health or contacting ConnexOntario can provide adults with additional guidance on how to support young people through family mental health and addiction struggles.

If you are trying to find the right words to use with a child in your life, or if you are feeling overwhelmed by a family crisis, you do not have to figure it out alone. You can talk through your specific situation in a calm, private space by visiting the Liam's Call chat.

Sources referenced: Kids Help Phone, Centre for Addiction and Mental Health, ConnexOntario, and SAMHSA.
